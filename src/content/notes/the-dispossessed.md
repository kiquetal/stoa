Le Guin's masterpiece is an *ambiguous utopia* — the subtitle refuses to let
either world win. The novel's structure alternates chapters between Anarres and
Urras, mirroring the physicist Shevek's own theory of simultaneity.

## Pravic and the shape of thought

The invented language *Pravic* structurally discourages possessive constructions.
You do not say "my hand" but "the hand." This is Sapir-Whorf as narrative
machinery: the language *is* the political philosophy, enforced at the level of
grammar.

> "You cannot buy the Revolution. You cannot make the Revolution. You can only
> be the Revolution. It is in your spirit, or it is nowhere."

A quiet, essential re-read.
